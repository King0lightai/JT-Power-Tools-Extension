Power User

Land email on the job.

Every JobTread job gets its own email address. BCC anyone you want — customer, sub, inspector, supplier — and the message auto-posts to that job's activity feed as an internal-team comment. Subject becomes the title, signatures get stripped, attachments come along, reply threads stay grouped.

Video walkthrough coming soon

What it does

JobTread tracks email replies to its own notifications, but it can't capture a thread you start from Outlook or Gmail. So the email that actually moves the job — the homeowner's "are we still on for Thursday?", the sub's revised bid, the inspector's red-tag photo — lives in your inbox while the job record stays half-empty. Email-to-Job fixes that.

Each job gets a unique address like pat-12345@jtptbills.com. Add it as a BCC on any email from anywhere. The message lands on the job's activity feed as a team-internal comment (customers and trade partners don't see it), with:

  • Subject as the comment title — easy to scan in the feed.
  • Signature stripped — no "Sent from my iPhone" / boilerplate sign-off / quoted reply chain.
  • Attachments included — PDFs, photos, contracts upload to JobTread and attach to the comment.
  • Reply threads grouped — when someone replies to a thread you already landed, the reply nests under the original comment instead of becoming a new parallel thread.
  • Forwarded threads exploded — forward a 6-message back-and-forth and the parser splits it: your intro lands as the parent comment, each historical message as a nested reply with its own From / Sent / Subject header.

Turn it on (admin / owner only)

Email-to-Job is opt-in per company. An owner or admin on your license flips a single toggle from the JT Power Tools Portal — that decision applies to everyone on the team.

  1. Sign in at app.jtpowertools.com.
  2. Open the Email to Job section in the portal sidebar.
  3. Flip the Company-wide toggle to ON.

That's it. Any Power User on your team can now provision per-job email addresses from inside the extension.

Provision an address for a job

  1. Open the JobTread job in your browser.
  2. Click the JT Power Tools icon in the toolbar. The popup shows a This Job's Email card with the address (auto-generated from the first 3 letters of the job name + its number — e.g. pat-12345@jtptbills.com).
  3. Click the copy icon to grab the address.
  4. Paste it into a custom field on the job (we use one called "Job Email") so the whole team can find it. Or add it directly to your address book / contact group.

The first popup open per job creates the address; every subsequent open is a fast read. Each address is unique to its job — no chance of one job's email landing on another.

Use it on a real email

  1. Compose an email in Outlook / Gmail / Apple Mail — anywhere.
  2. BCC the job's address. (BCC, not CC — the recipient never sees it.)
  3. Send.
  4. Open the job's Activity feed in JobTread. Your message is there, posted as a team-internal comment.

If the customer or sub later replies to that thread (without BCC'ing the job address), their reply also lands — as long as it's part of the same conversation. The parser uses the email's threading headers to find the original comment and nest the reply under it.

Forwarded threads

You can also forward an existing thread to the job address — say a 6-message back-and-forth that started before you knew about Email-to-Job. The parser handles common forward formats (Outlook, Gmail, Apple Mail) and splits them:

  • Your intro (whatever you typed above the quoted thread) becomes the parent comment, with the email's subject as the title.
  • Each historical message becomes a nested reply under that parent, with its own From / Sent / Subject header inline.

Capped at 20 segments per forward to bound spam. If the parser can't find thread markers, it falls back to a single comment with the whole body.

Privacy & visibility

  • Team-only. Every comment created by Email-to-Job is posted with team-internal visibility. Your customers and trade partners do not see them on their job portals.
  • Signature stripping. The parser removes boilerplate sign-offs ("Best, Pat" / "Sent from my iPhone" / etc.) and quoted reply chains before posting. The comment shows only what was said in the most recent message.
  • Spam mitigation. Each address embeds a random token. If you start getting spam on one, an admin can rotate the token from the portal — keeps the job address but invalidates the old one.
  • Forensic R2 storage. Every original MIME message is stored in Cloudflare R2 for audit. Disabling the feature stops new mail and blocks new provisioning instantly but leaves existing parked emails + history intact.

Good to know

  • Power User tier required for individual users to provision per-job addresses. Free / Pro tier members on the same license can still view the resulting comments inside JobTread — they just can't generate the address themselves.
  • One address per job, persistent for the life of the job. Rotation is admin-only and rare.
  • Custom alias support. Don't like the auto-generated pat-12345@ format? Admins can set a vanity alias per job via the MCP server. (UI for this is coming.)
  • Inbound only. The address receives mail; it doesn't send. To send a JT comment as an email to someone, use JobTread's existing comment-with-recipients flow.
  • Soft kill. Disabling the company-wide toggle later doesn't delete anything. All historical parked emails and posted comments stay where they are. Flip the toggle back ON and provisioning resumes.

Troubleshooting

  • The address card doesn't appear in the popup. Three things gate it: (1) you're signed in to the extension, (2) you're on a Power User tier, (3) your license admin has enabled the company toggle in the portal. If any of those are missing, the card stays hidden.
  • Email sent but didn't land on the job. Check that you BCC'd the exact address (it's case-insensitive but spelling matters). Also confirm with your admin that Email-to-Job is enabled. If both check out, contact support — the original message is preserved in R2 so we can trace any drop.
  • Reply landed as a new parent comment instead of nesting. The threading is based on the email's In-Reply-To header. Some webmail clients strip it on replies. The fallback is a parallel comment — content is still captured, just not visually grouped.
